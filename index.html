OUR ROOTS

The History of Boyds, Maryland

From a Railroad Village to a Growing Community

Boyds, Maryland, is a historic community in Montgomery County with a history shaped by farming, railroads, and the people who built their lives here. Although Boyds is now part of a growing region near Germantown and Clarksburg, it still preserves important reminders of its rural past.

Before the 1800s

Before Boyds Became a Village

Long before European settlers arrived, Indigenous peoples, including the Seneca and Piscataway, traveled through the area. By the 1700s, European settlement and tobacco farming had transformed parts of the landscape. Enslaved African Americans were forced to work on local plantations, an important and difficult part of the area's early history.

1873

The Railroad Changes Everything

Boyds began developing into a village in 1873, when Scottish railroad contractor James Alexander Boyd helped build the Baltimore and Ohio Railroad's Metropolitan Branch through the area. He purchased land near the tracks, and a settlement known as Boyd's Station grew around the railroad.

The railway connected the community to other towns and cities, helping local businesses grow. Stores, mills, homes, and other services appeared, turning a rural area into a small but active village. The railroad became central to everyday life, carrying passengers, goods, and mail.

After the Civil War

The Historic Community of White Grounds

Boyds' history also includes White Grounds, an African American community established after the Civil War by formerly enslaved people who purchased land near the former Bucklodge Plantation. Residents built homes, churches, a school, and other community institutions.

One important surviving landmark is the Boyds Negro School, built in 1895. It served African American students in the area from 1895 to 1936, during a time when public education was segregated. Today, the school stands as a reminder of the community's determination to build educational opportunities for its children.

20th Century

The Twentieth Century and Beyond

As transportation and development changed Montgomery County, Boyds gradually evolved. The railroad continued to serve the area, while farms and historic buildings remained important features of the landscape.

In 1984, nearby Little Seneca Lake was completed, creating a major local water resource and a destination for outdoor recreation. Parks, trails, and natural areas have since become part of what makes the wider Boyds area distinctive.

Boyds' historic district was recognized in Montgomery County's historic preservation planning in 1985, helping protect its character and connections to the past.

Today

Boyds Today

Today, Boyds combines its rural heritage with the changes taking place throughout Montgomery County. Historic homes, community landmarks, open spaces, and the continuing commuter rail connection tell the story of a place shaped by generations of residents.

Understanding Boyds' history means remembering all the people who contributed to it—from Indigenous communities and agricultural workers to railroad builders, formerly enslaved people, local families, and today's residents.

Boyds is more than a place on the map. It is a community with a past worth preserving and a future still being written.
